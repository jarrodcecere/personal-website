Instructional Design · Higher Education

Designing learning experiences where pedagogy and technology meet.

I’m Jarrod Cecere, an Instructional Designer with the Teaching, Learning and Technology Center at Seton Hall University. I work with faculty to design effective learning experiences, solve instructional challenges, and explore practical ways to use emerging technologies in teaching.

Explore my work

My work spans instructional design, faculty development, AI in education, immersive learning, and experiential education—grounded in psychology and years of classroom teaching.

Course Design & Faculty Consultation

Designing effective learning experiences with faculty.

My instructional design work begins with the learning need—not the technology. I collaborate with faculty on course structure, assignments, engagement, accessibility, assessment, and the thoughtful use of instructional tools across online, hybrid, and in-person learning.

Selected Projects

Instructional design in practice.

A selection of projects that show how I work with faculty to solve instructional challenges and put new approaches and technologies into practice.

Immersive Learning · Biochemistry

Making Structural Biology Visible

Partnered with faculty to address the challenge of making complex molecular structures more visible and interactive. I helped identify Nanome VR as an instructional solution, tested its capabilities, and supported its integration into a Biochemistry of Metabolism course alongside 3D-printed molecular models. I later documented the collaboration in a Seton Hall technology article.

Experiential Learning · Innovation Hub

Pre-College Students Put Concepts Into Practice

Collaborated with faculty and program partners to develop Innovation Hub experiences that helped pre-college students apply academic concepts through robotics, 3D printing, prototyping and other emerging technologies. I documented the work in an August 2026 Seton Hall technology article.

About

Learning first. Technology with a purpose.

That background still shapes how I approach learning design: start with the learner and the instructional goal, then choose the methods and technologies that best support them.
